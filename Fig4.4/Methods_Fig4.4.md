# Statistical analysis – Figure 4.4 (progression pattern and post-progression cause of death)

**Population and time scale.** The analysis was restricted to patients with a first radiological progression (death without progression excluded). Patients flagged for exclusion in the cause-of-death review were removed: non-HCC histology, another malignancy, external combined treatment or prolonged loss to follow-up; flags marked "can be included" were kept. One patient with an inconsistent progression date (PFS > OS) was also removed. Time zero was the date of first progression. Follow-up ran to death or last contact (data cut-off 30 September 2025). The three treatment groups were analysed together. Every model was stratified by treatment group (separate baseline hazards) and adjusted for time to progression (TTP, months). The baseline covariates were age (per 10 years), sex, HBV aetiology, ECOG PS (0/1/2), ALBI grade (1/2a vs 2b/3), AFP ≥400 ng/mL, PIVKA-II ≥1000 mAU/mL, tumour number >3, tumour size (≤5, 5–10, >10 cm, infiltrative), portal-vein invasion (absent, Vp1–2, Vp3–4), hepatic-vein tumour thrombus, extrahepatic spread and cirrhosis. Nine patients had missing Vp class. They were assigned "absent" when PVTT was absent and otherwise the modal Vp class. Post-baseline variables (subsequent therapy, status at progression) were not adjusted for, because they may lie on the causal pathway.

**Progression pattern.** First progression was recorded as one or more components: intrahepatic lesion growth (IHG), tumour-thrombus upgrade, new intrahepatic lesions (NIH), new vascular invasion, extrahepatic lesion growth (EHG), new extrahepatic metastases (NEH) and systemic tumour growth (STG). Tumour-thrombus upgrade was counted as IHG and new vascular invasion as NIH. Each progression was then classified by site and form. Site was systemic (Sys) when STG was present or when both intrahepatic and extrahepatic components were present, and otherwise intrahepatic (IH) or extrahepatic (EH). Form was tumour growth (TG) when only growth components (IHG, EHG, STG) were present, new lesions (NL) when only new-lesion components (NIH, NEH) were present, and TG+NL when both were present. Crossing site and form gave nine patterns (IH-TG, IH-NL, IH-TG+NL, EH-TG, EH-NL, EH-TG+NL, Sys-TG, Sys-NL, Sys-TG+NL).

**Outcomes.** Deaths were classified as intrahepatic HCC-related, extrahepatic HCC-related, systemic HCC-related, non-HCC (liver-related, treatment-related and other causes) or undetermined (unclassified HCC-related, unknown or missing). Undetermined deaths were kept as a separate competing event. This ensured that the cause-specific cumulative incidences summed to all-cause mortality. They were excluded in a sensitivity analysis.

**Absolute risk (4.4A).** A cause-specific Cox model was fitted for each cause. The covariates were pattern, TTP and the baseline covariates, stratified by treatment group, with Breslow ties. The undetermined cause was modelled with a stratum-specific Nelson–Aalen estimator. Individual cumulative incidence functions were obtained as F_j(t|x) = Σ_{s≤t} S(s−|x) dΛ_j(s|x), with S = exp(−Σ_j Λ_j). We confirmed numerically that this implementation agrees with riskRegression::predictRisk. Standardised (G-computation) cumulative incidences F̄_kj(t) were obtained by setting every progressor's pattern to k, keeping their own covariates and treatment group, and averaging the predicted risks. The primary time point was 12 months after progression; 6 and 24 months were secondary. From the same quantities we derived three further measures:
- the absolute risk difference against the population-standardised average, ARD_kj = F̄_kj − Σ_l w_l F̄_lj, with w_l the observed pattern proportions;
- the cause composition, Q_kj = F̄_kj / F̄_k,all;
- a CIF-based proportional mortality ratio, PMR_kj = Q_kj / Q_·j.

PMR is a proportion measure, not a risk measure, and is interpreted alongside the absolute cumulative incidences. Ninety-five percent confidence intervals were patient-level non-parametric bootstrap percentile intervals: 1000 resamples, with all models refitted and all quantities computed within the same resample. Cells with no events by the time point are reported as "0 events" without a model estimate. Unadjusted Aalen–Johansen estimates are given for comparison.

**Relative risk (4.4B).** Pattern effects were expressed relative to systemic tumour growth (Sys-TG). We used Cox models for all-cause death and cause-specific Cox models for each cause (csHR, primary, aetiological). Fine–Gray subdistribution hazard ratios (sHR) were estimated by weighted Cox regression on data expanded with survival::finegray. That approach used treatment-group-specific censoring distributions, the same stratification and robust variance. Cells with fewer than 3 events, or columns whose reference cell had fewer than 3 events, were prespecified as not estimable (NE). For each outcome, an overall Wald test of the eight pattern terms (df = 8) was reported; this test does not depend on the reference category. Individual HRs are exploratory, and Benjamini–Hochberg q values are reported. Proportional hazards were assessed with scaled Schoenfeld residuals.

**Cross-cause heterogeneity.** Heterogeneity of pattern effects across the three HCC-related causes was tested with the Lunn–McNeil data-augmentation approach. Each patient contributed one record per cause; non-HCC and undetermined deaths were treated as censored. The model was stratified by cause × treatment group, and all covariates interacted with cause. A likelihood-ratio test compared a model with common pattern effects against one with cause-specific pattern effects (df = 16). As more robust, lower-dimensional tests we also report site × cause and form × cause interactions (each df = 4). When the overall test was significant, each pattern's effect was expressed relative to the pattern-weighted average using weighted effect coding. Equality of that effect across the three causes was then tested with a Wald test (df = 2), with Holm correction over nine patterns; these tests mirror the PMR descriptively.

**Validation and sensitivity analyses.** The models were refitted with IH-TG as reference to reproduce the previously reported matrix. Sensitivity analyses comprised:
- 6- and 24-month estimates and unadjusted Aalen–Johansen estimates;
- Firth-penalised Cox models (coxphf, with treatment group as a covariate because coxphf does not support strata) for sparse cells;
- exclusion of undetermined deaths;
- inclusion of the flagged patients;
- likelihood-ratio tests of site × treatment-group and pattern × treatment-group interactions for post-progression all-cause death, supporting the pooled analysis.

Analyses used R 4.3.3 with survival 3.5-8, riskRegression 2023.12.21, prodlim, coxphf 1.13.4 and ggplot2. Two-sided P < 0.05 was considered significant.
